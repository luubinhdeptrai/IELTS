# 100 Daily English Sentences for Speaking and Listening

## 1. How to Use This File

This file is designed for **active speaking**, not passive reading. Learn a small group of sentences at a time and follow this cycle:

1. Read the English sentence and understand the situation.
2. Listen to a clear recording of the sentence if possible.
3. Repeat it slowly three times, then at natural speed three times.
4. Cover the English and say it from the Vietnamese meaning.
5. Use the sentence in a short response or a new situation.
6. Review it later the same day and again the next day.

Do not try to memorize all 100 sentences at once. Aim to make a few sentences automatic each day.

## 2. Learning Principles

* **Recall before checking:** Try to speak before looking at the answer.
* **Use whole chunks:** Learn `I don't know` as one sound pattern, not three separate words.
* **Speak aloud:** Silent reading does not train your mouth or response speed.
* **Copy rhythm:** Notice stressed words, contractions, linking, and intonation.
* **Personalize:** Change one word to make each sentence true for you.
* **Review repeatedly:** Short daily reviews are more effective than one long session.
* **Accept simple English:** A short natural sentence is better than a long translated sentence.

## 4. Useful Sentence Patterns

### Group 1: Greetings and Small Talk

**Pattern:** `How + be + subject?` / `What + be + possessive noun?`

* How are you?
* How's your family?
* What's your name?

Use these patterns to begin a friendly conversation or ask for basic personal information.

### Group 2: Asking Simple Questions

**Pattern:** `Question word + be + subject?`

* What is this?
* Who is that?
* Where are you?

Change the question word according to the information you need.

### Group 3: Talking About Feelings

**Pattern:** `I'm + adjective.` / `I feel + adjective.`

* I'm tired.
* I'm nervous.
* I feel better.

Use an adjective after `be` or `feel` to describe your condition or emotion.

### Group 4: Talking About Needs

**Pattern:** `I need + noun.` / `I want to + verb.`

* I need some help.
* I need a break.
* I want to sit down.

Use `need` for something necessary and `want` for something you would like.

### Group 5: Asking for Help

**Pattern:** `Can you + base verb?`

* Can you help me?
* Can you show me?
* Can you carry this?

Add `please` or use `Could you ...?` to sound more polite.

### Group 6: Making Requests

**Pattern:** `Could you + base verb?` / `Can I have + noun?`

* Could you speak slowly?
* Could you wait here?
* Can I have some water?

Use these patterns for polite everyday requests.

### Group 7: Saying Yes, No, and Maybe

**Pattern:** `I'm not + adjective.` / `Maybe + time.`

* I'm not sure.
* I'm not ready.
* Maybe tomorrow.

These patterns help you avoid giving an answer that is too direct.

### Group 8: Apologizing and Thanking

**Pattern:** `Thanks for + noun/-ing.` / `Sorry + clause.`

* Thanks for your help.
* Thanks for waiting.
* Sorry I'm late.

Use `for + noun/-ing` to say exactly what you appreciate.

### Group 9: Talking About Daily Routines

**Pattern:** `I'm + verb-ing.`

* I'm getting ready.
* I'm having lunch.
* I'm studying English.

Use the present continuous for something happening now or around now.

### Group 10: Talking About Likes and Dislikes

**Pattern:** `I like/love/don't like + noun.`

* I like this place.
* I love this song.
* I don't like spicy food.

Replace the noun to talk about food, music, places, activities, or things.

### Group 11: Asking for Clarification

**Pattern:** `How do you + base verb ...?` / `Can you + base verb + that?`

* How do you say this in English?
* How do you spell that?
* Can you repeat that?

Use these questions whenever communication becomes unclear.

### Group 12: Reacting Naturally

**Pattern:** `That's + adjective/noun phrase.`

* That's great.
* That's too bad.
* That's a good idea.

Short reactions show that you are listening and make conversation more natural.

### Group 13: Shopping and Ordering

**Pattern:** `Can I get + noun, please?` / `How much is + noun?`

* Can I get a coffee, please?
* Can I get a sandwich, please?
* How much is this shirt?

Use `Can I get ...?` in shops, cafes, and casual restaurants.

### Group 14: Phone and Messages

**Pattern:** `I'll + base verb + you + back.`

* I'll call you back.
* I'll text you back.
* I'll get back to you.

Use this pattern when you will respond later.

### Group 15: Ending a Conversation

**Pattern:** `Have a + adjective + noun.`

* Have a good day.
* Have a nice weekend.
* Have a safe trip.

Use this friendly pattern when saying goodbye.

## 5. Mini-Dialogues for Practice

### Dialogue 1: Meeting Someone

* A: Hi, how are you?
* B: I'm good, thanks. How are you?
* A: Pretty good.

### Dialogue 2: First Introduction

* A: What's your name?
* B: My name's Minh.
* A: Nice to meet you.
* B: Nice to meet you too.

### Dialogue 3: Getting Ready

* A: Are you ready?
* B: Not yet. I'm getting ready.
* A: Okay. Take your time.

### Dialogue 4: Feeling Hungry

* A: I'm hungry.
* B: Me too. Let's eat.
* A: Good idea.

### Dialogue 5: Feeling Unwell

* A: I'm not feeling well.
* B: That's too bad. You should rest.
* A: I will. Thank you.

### Dialogue 6: Asking for Help

* A: Excuse me. Can you help me?
* B: Of course. What do you need?
* A: Can you show me how to open this?

### Dialogue 7: Asking Someone to Repeat

* A: The meeting starts at nine fifteen.
* B: Could you say that again?
* A: Sure. It starts at nine fifteen.

### Dialogue 8: Needing More Time

* A: Are you done?
* B: Not yet. I just need a minute.

## 6. Speaking Practice Method

### The Six-Step Speaking Cycle

1. **Understand:** Read the situation and Vietnamese meaning once.
2. **Repeat slowly:** Say the English sentence three times with clear sounds.
3. **Repeat naturally:** Say it three times with contractions and natural rhythm.
4. **Recall:** Hide the English and produce it from the situation or Vietnamese cue.
5. **Transform:** Change one word, such as `I'm tired` to `I'm hungry`.
6. **Respond:** Ask someone to give the common response, or play both roles yourself.

### Recording Yourself

* Record five sentences on your phone.
* Compare your recording with a reliable English audio source.
* Check stressed words, final consonants, contractions, and overall rhythm.
* Record the same sentences again without reading.
* Keep the second recording and compare it with a new recording one week later.

### Avoid Translating Word by Word

Connect the English sentence directly to a situation or image. For example, imagine arriving late while saying `Sorry I'm late.` The real-life situation should become the cue for the English sentence.

## 7. Listening Practice Method

Use text-to-speech, a dictionary recording, a teacher, or a study partner to make audio prompts.

1. **Listen and repeat:** Hear one sentence and copy it immediately.
2. **Listen and write:** Hear it twice and write exactly what you hear.
3. **Listen and choose:** Choose the correct Vietnamese meaning from two or three options.
4. **Listen and respond:** Hear `Are you ready?` and answer without reading.
5. **Slow then normal:** Begin at 0.75 speed only when necessary, then return to normal speed.
6. **Mixed review:** Play sentences in random order so you respond to meaning, not list position.

These patterns occur frequently in speech. Repeated exposure helps your brain recognize contractions and whole chunks before you consciously translate them.

## 8. Shadowing Practice Method

### Basic Shadowing Routine

1. Listen once without speaking.
2. Listen while reading the sentence.
3. Pause and repeat the sentence three times.
4. Play it again and speak at the same time as the audio five times.
5. Turn away from the text and say it from memory.
6. Use the same pattern in a new context.

### What to Focus On

* Copy the speaker's stressed words and pauses.
* Keep unstressed words short and light.
* Use contractions such as `I'm`, `don't`, and `I'll` naturally.
* Copy the rising tone of yes/no questions.
* Finish final sounds clearly enough to distinguish words.

### Signs of Improvement

You are improving when you can stay close to the audio without stopping, use a similar rhythm, recall the sentence without reading, and respond appropriately within two seconds.

## 9. Pronunciation and Contraction Notes

### Safe Everyday Contractions

| Full Form | Normal Spoken Form | Example | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| I am | I'm | I'm tired. | Use in speaking and most informal writing. |
| you are | you're | You're welcome. | Do not confuse it with `your`. |
| do not | don't | I don't know. | Keep the final /t/ light but recognizable. |
| cannot | can't | I can't hear you. | UK and US vowel sounds differ. |
| I will | I'll | I'll call you back. | Usually pronounced as one syllable. |
| what is | what's | What's your name? | Very common in speech. |
| how is | how's | How's it going? | Very common in casual questions. |
| that is | that's | That's great. | Link the two words into one beat. |

### Common Casual Reductions

| Written Form | Casual Sound | Example | Beginner Advice |
|---|---|---|---|
| want to | wanna | I wanna go home. | Learn to recognize it; write `want to` in normal writing. |
| going to | gonna | I'm gonna call her. | Common in casual speech; avoid it in formal writing. |
| let me | lemme | Lemme check. | Recognize it first; `Let me` is always safe to use. |
| have to | hafta | I hafta go. | It is a natural sound reduction, not standard spelling. |
| did you | didja | Didja get my message? | Recognize it in listening; pronounce clearly while learning. |

Do not force casual reductions. Clear contractions such as `I'm` and `don't` are the best starting point.

## 10. Common Mistakes Vietnamese Learners Make

| Mistake Type | Incorrect | Correct | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| Missing subject | Am tired. | I'm tired. | English normally requires a subject. |
| Missing `be` | I hungry. | I'm hungry. | An adjective needs `be` after the subject. |
| Wrong `be` verb | You is ready? | Are you ready? | Use `are` with `you`. |
| Statement word order in a question | You are where? | Where are you? | Put the question word first, then the verb. |
| Missing auxiliary | What you mean? | What do you mean? | Present-simple questions usually need `do`. |
| Direct translation | I very like it. | I really like it. | `Very` does not normally modify the verb `like`. |
| Wrong request form | You help me? | Can you help me? | Use `can/could` for a natural request. |
| Overly formal daily speech | I sincerely apologize for my tardiness. | Sorry I'm late. | Choose a short natural sentence in casual situations. |
| Avoiding contractions | I do not know. | I don't know. | The contraction sounds more natural in conversation. |
| Dropping final sounds | I miss your call. | I missed your call. | The final `-ed` shows past time. |
| Confusing `your/you're` | Your welcome. | You're welcome. | `You're` means `you are`. |
| Flat question tone | Are you ready. | Are you ready? | A gentle rise helps signal a yes/no question. |

## 14. Final Daily Practice Routine

Use this **15-minute routine** after completing the 14-day plan:

- [ ] **Minutes 1-3:** Review ten old sentences from Vietnamese or situation cues.
- [ ] **Minutes 4-6:** Listen to five sentences and write what you hear.

The goal is not perfect pronunciation after one session. The goal is to understand a situation and produce a useful English sentence quickly, clearly, and confidently.
